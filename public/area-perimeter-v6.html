Teacher guide · Area & Perimeter

Teaching sequence

This engine follows the same learning progression as the hands-on Singapore Measurement Mission: begin by building the measurement with concrete unit tools, move to guided calculation, then remove the scaffold for independent practice.

Area

Build it: pupils cover the rectangle with 1 cm² or 1 m² tiles, according to the selected unit. Guided: they see rows and columns and connect repeated addition to multiplication. Independent: they receive only the rectangle dimensions.

Area of a rectilinear figure

Build it: pupils cover only the L-shaped region with 1 cm² or 1 m² tiles. Guided: a split line separates the figure into two rectangles; pupils find the missing height, calculate the area of each rectangle and add the two areas. Independent: the split line and helper calculation are removed, so pupils decide how to partition the figure before finding the total area.

Area of figures with half-squares

This task is only about finding area from a square grid. The figure contains whole squares and triangular half-squares. Half-squares are always generated in pairs, so every final answer is a whole number. Build it: whole squares and half-squares are labelled to support counting. Guided: the number of whole squares and half-squares is shown and pupils combine two halves to make one whole square. Independent: only the grid figure is shown.

Perimeter

Build it: pupils place 1-unit ruler segments around the outside. Guided: all four side lengths and the addition sentence are shown. Independent: only length and breadth are labelled.

Rectilinear perimeter

Build it: pupils trace the complete L-shaped boundary with 1-unit ruler segments. Guided: two missing sides are worked out from aligned horizontal and vertical lengths before all six sides are added. Independent: the missing sides remain marked ? and pupils find them before calculating the perimeter.

Classroom language

  • Area is the amount of surface covered by square units.
  • Perimeter is the total distance around the outside of a shape.
  • For a rectilinear figure, aligned horizontal lengths and aligned vertical lengths can be used to find missing sides.
  • Area uses square units such as cm² or m². Perimeter uses length units such as cm or m.
