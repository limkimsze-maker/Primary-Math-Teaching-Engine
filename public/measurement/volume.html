Teacher guide: Volume Measurement Engine

Try your first activity

  1. Open Examples and click First steps. This gives a 500 ml beaker with 50 ml intervals and 5 questions.
  2. In Setup, choose whether pupils read a liquid level, fill to a given volume, compare two volumes, order three volumes, find the total volume, or practise a mixture.
  3. Choose Beakers per question: 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5. With 1 beaker, pupils can practise reading or filling. With 2–5 beakers, the activity becomes a total-volume task automatically: one beaker is partly filled to a marked volume and every other beaker is full to capacity. Pupils add all the liquid and enter one total.
  4. Choose the Scale unit and Beaker capacity. The large ml or ℓ on the diagram tells pupils what the numbered marks mean.
  5. In Scale, choose the Smallest interval and spacing of Numbered marks. Begin with 50 ml spaces and 100 ml numbered marks. On litre scales, numbered marks stay in whole litres and smaller intervals are stated in ml.
  6. Choose answers in ml, ℓ and ml, or let pupils choose between them. For a volume below 1 ℓ, use 0 in the ℓ box. The ml part must be 0–999. Filling questions use the liquid level instead of answer boxes.
  7. Leave interval support and visual hints available at first. Click Generate activity, then Pupil view. Pupil view applies changed settings; if they are unchanged, it keeps the current practice session.
  8. Pupils click Check answer, retry if needed, and use Next question after a correct answer. Help me offers up to three hints. For filling, drag the liquid level or use the slider, +/− buttons or arrow keys. Teacher view returns to the builder.

What to teach

  • Read the liquid, not the rim. Capacity tells us the maximum marked volume. The volume of liquid is the reading at the blue level line. A 500 ml beaker can contain only 250 ml.
  • Find one small interval. Between 100 ml and 200 ml, 2 equal spaces mean each space is 50 ml: 100 ÷ 2 = 50. Count spaces, not marks.
  • Read upwards from zero. Start at a numbered mark below the liquid level, count spaces and add their values.
  • Read at eye level. These diagrams use a flat level line. Use a real beaker to demonstrate viewing a liquid level at eye level.
  • Match the answer unit. 1000 ml = 1 ℓ. A volume of 1500 ml is 1 ℓ 500 ml. For an exact number of litres, use 0 in the ml box.
  • Compare and order volumes. Read each beaker first. For two beakers, identify the greater volume. For three beakers, arrange A, B and C from least to greatest.

Build a total such as 4 ℓ 250 ml

  1. In Examples, choose 4 ℓ 250 ml example. This shows four full 1 ℓ beakers and a smaller 500 ml beaker holding 250 ml.
  2. In Setup, choose Find the total volume, 5 beakers and Set each beaker separately. You can also choose fewer beakers.
  3. In Scale, open each beaker’s settings. Set its unit, capacity, smallest interval and numbered marks. Use Use A for all beakers to copy A’s setup, then change the small beaker separately.
  4. Choose each liquid level: Random each question, Full to the top mark, or Set a reading. Fixed readings are entered in whole ml; 1000 means 1 ℓ. For the small beaker, enter 250.
  5. Choose answers in ml, ℓ and ml, or let pupils choose. Pupils add all the displayed liquid volumes and enter one total: 4250 ml or 4 ℓ 250 ml.
  6. For an exact question, choose 1 question. For repeated practice, choose 4 large + 1 small practice: four full 1 ℓ beakers stay constant while the small beaker’s reading changes. Whole litres can be added first, followed by the remaining ml.

The simple diagrams use illustrative sizes; always use the printed scales and capacities to read each volume.

A clear classroom display: Setup, Scale and Examples are separate tabs. On a typical laptop, the diagram and answer controls stay in their own areas, and Generate activity remains visible at the bottom of the builder. Feedback and hints do not move the diagram. Smaller screens, enlarged text or long help text can still need scrolling. Reading, filling and comparison use two columns on larger screens. Total-volume activities use compact beakers in one row where there is space; a narrower display can need scrolling within the diagram area. On phones, beakers stack or use two columns to keep the scales readable. Three or more beakers may need scrolling within the diagram area on a shorter laptop screen; the answer controls stay in place, and the next highlighted beaker is brought into view. What does “Download activity” save?

It saves your current settings, including any fixed liquid readings you set.

Think of it as Download configured engine: an HTML copy ready to use with the settings you chose. Open it in a browser: it starts in Pupil view and works without internet.

For example, choose a 500 ml beaker, 50 ml intervals and 8 questions. The file keeps those settings, including each beaker’s setup. Beakers set to Random each question get new random readings whenever it opens or restarts. Beakers set to Full or Set a reading keep their readings. If every beaker is full or fixed, the question stays the same. Pupils opening the same file may get different questions when random readings are enabled.

  • Download again with the same settings: the same activity setup; random readings can differ, while full and fixed readings stay the same.
  • Change settings, then download: the new copy uses the changed settings.
  • The online engine is updated: older downloaded files stay unchanged. Download a new copy to get the latest improvements.

Share the HTML file for practice. Each copy includes the teacher guide and uses simple original diagrams.

Save and load settings: Save settings downloads only the setup as a JSON file. Load settings restores it in the engine. A JSON settings file is not a runnable activity and does not include pupil results.

Use the summary: At the end, review first-try answers, retries and hints used. These help plan follow-up teaching. The summary covers this session only; it is not a saved class report or a mastery grade.
